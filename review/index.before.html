Routing tiers

Tiering means sending each request to the cheapest model that's good enough for it, instead of one model for everything. A tier is one slice of that traffic: pick its model, its entry share, and its workload weight — hover a tier's ? for what each class is for. Tier order (T1 → Tn) is the escalation path: requests that fail a tier are retried one tier up, and the failed attempt still gets billed. Cache and batch controls follow the selected model's pricing rules.

New to tiered routing? A 30-second primer

Most requests don't need the most capable model. Tiered routing splits traffic by difficulty: a cheap high-volume tier handles routine work (Pawns), mid-priced workhorses cover everyday tasks (Knights), flagship models take the heavy lifting (Rooks), and frontier models are saved for the hardest, highest-stakes problems (Queen). Requests that a tier can't handle escalate to the next tier up — you pay for the failed attempt and the retry, which is why the escalation rate is one of the levers below. Because prices differ 10–25× between tiers, the traffic mix — not the vendor — is the biggest cost decision you make. The chess names encode the logic: a set gives you eight pawns and one queen, and a healthy routing mix looks the same. The bar at the bottom shows what your mix costs versus sending everything to the Queen.

Cache semantics by provider: Anthropic bills cache writes (5-minute and 1-hour TTLs) and reads; OpenAI caches automatically with no write fee; Mistral bills cached reads at 10% of input via prompt_cache_key with no write fee. Batch: explicit published prices are used where available; otherwise the provider's stated discount is applied and the figure is labeled an estimate. Cache discounts are assumed to stack with batch pricing (verified for Anthropic; treated as an assumption elsewhere). Not modeled: long-context surcharges (flagged on affected tiers), regional-routing uplifts, tool/server-side tokens. Escalation: tier order (T1 → Tn) is the escalation path; failed attempts are billed in full at their tier, escalated requests are re-billed at the destination tier's token profile with the payload overhead applied to input, and escalated volume is not batch-eligible. The all-queen baseline assumes a single model handles each entry request once, with no escalation.
